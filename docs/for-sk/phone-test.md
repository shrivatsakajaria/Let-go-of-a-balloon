# The 5-minute phone test

Do this on your own phone, once the site is live. It checks the things the automatic tests cannot: how it looks, how it feels, and how smooth it is on a real phone.

You need about 5 minutes, a phone with a good signal, and somewhere to note anything odd.

## Before you start

- Use your phone's own browser: **Safari** on an iPhone, **Chrome** on Android. Do not open it from inside the LinkedIn or X app for this test.
- Open this address: **https://let-go-of-a-balloon.pages.dev/?fps=1**
  The `?fps=1` on the end adds a small frame counter at the top of the screen. It shows how smooth the 3D is. 50 or more is smooth. Under 25 is jerky.
- If I sent you a different address (for example, a preview), use that one instead, with `?fps=1` on the end.

The landing card (the postcard at the end) is being redesigned. If it appears, close it and carry on. Do not judge it yet.

## 1. London (about 2 minutes)

**Tap:** nothing yet. Just look.

**You should see,** within about 2 seconds:

- the title "Let go of a balloon" and a sky;
- then the 3D streets of Westminster, with a person holding a red balloon in the middle of the screen and Big Ben behind them;
- "From London" above an orange **Let go** button, and "Use my location" below it;
- a row of city names you can slide sideways;
- small print at the very bottom: the simulation notice and the map credits.

**Tap:** **Let go**.

**You should see:**

- the buttons fade away within a second;
- the person opens their hand, and the balloon rises past the windows, the lamp posts and the roofs;
- no flat grey band or blank block across the picture while the camera rises (the test machine showed one once; it may only be the machine);
- within about 40 seconds the camera settles, and a bar appears at the top (local time, height, speed, and "Over ...") and at the bottom ("Day 1 of 3", **Show me the whole trip**);
- the frame counter staying at 50 or more for most of the time.

**Tap:** **Ride**, then **Above**, then **Side-on**. Each one changes the camera. Then tap **Film** to go back.

**Tap:** **Show me the whole trip**.

**You should see:** a high, wide view. The route draws across the map for about 20 seconds, with place names as it passes them, labelled "Forecast route, 72 hours in 20 seconds". At the end, tap **Back to live** (or **Close**, if the card appears).

**Tap:** **Let go of another** to go back to the start screen.

## 2. Windermere: a small town with no landmark model (about 1 minute)

**Tap:** **From London**. A search box opens.

**Type:** `Windermere`. Wait for the list.

**You should see:** "Windermere, England, United Kingdom" in the list. **Tap** it.

**You should see:** the start screen again, now saying "From Windermere". Behind the person there are ordinary houses, the lake and the fells (hills). There is no famous building, and that is correct.

**Tap:** **Let go**. Watch until the camera settles.

**Check:**

- the balloon never goes into a hill;
- the lake looks like water, and the hills look like hills, not flat ground;
- the "Over ..." line names somewhere in the Lake District or near it.

**Tap:** **Let go of another**.

## 3. Auckland: crossing the date line (about 1 minute)

The date line is the line in the Pacific where the calendar jumps a day. Balloons from New Zealand often cross it. This is the case most likely to go wrong.

**Tap:** **From ...**, type `Auckland`, and tap "Auckland, Auckland, New Zealand" (or the closest match).

**Tap:** **Let go**. When the camera settles, tap **Show me the whole trip**.

**Check:**

- the route is one smooth line. It never jumps from one side of the screen to the other;
- if it heads east, out over the Pacific, it simply carries on past the date line. Nothing flickers and the map does not go blank;
- the time in the top bar still makes sense. Over the date line it may show the next or previous day, which is correct.

Then tap **Back to live**.

## 4. Reduced motion (about 1 minute)

Some people turn down the movement on their phone because it makes them feel ill. The site should calm down when they do.

**Turn the setting on.**

- **iPhone:** open **Settings**, tap **Accessibility**, tap **Motion**, then turn on **Reduce Motion**.
- **Android:** open **Settings**, tap **Accessibility**, and turn on **Remove animations**. On a Samsung it is under **Accessibility**, then **Visibility enhancements** (or **Colour and motion**). If you cannot find it, search Settings for "animations".

Go back to the browser and **reload the page** (pull down from the top, or tap the circular arrow).

**Tap:** **Let go**.

**You should see:**

- no sweeping camera move. Instead there is a still view of the person and the balloon rising, then a cut to the settled view;
- no thin white wind streaks once the balloon is high up.

**Tap:** **Show me the whole trip**.

**You should see:** no fly-through. The route simply draws itself in place, over about 2 seconds, labelled "Forecast route".

**Turn the setting off again** when you have finished.

## If something is wrong

Tell me in chat. The most useful message has three things:

1. **Which step**, for example "Step 3, Auckland".
2. **What you saw**, in your own words, for example "the line jumped to the other side of the screen".
3. **A screenshot.**
   - **iPhone:** press the side button and the volume-up button at the same time.
   - **Android:** press the power button and the volume-down button at the same time.

If you can, also send me the page's address from that moment: tap the address bar, then **Copy**, and paste it into the chat. After you let go, the address holds the whole balloon, so I can open exactly what you saw.

One thing to keep private: if you used **Use my location**, that address contains where you were standing. Only send it if you are happy for me to see that.

Please also tell me the phone you used (for example "iPhone 13") and the lowest number you saw on the frame counter.

If everything looked right, just say "phone test passed".
